How to play Petty Theft

A prop hunt for 1–10 players. Two teams take turns: one round as thieves hiding in plain sight as the furniture, the next round as the police trying to catch them.

A match

  1. Lobby. Share the four-letter room code. The host picks the map (or lets the crew vote), how many round wins take the match (first to 3 by default), and whether bots fill empty seats.
  2. Teams. Everyone is split at random into two teams, the Magpies and the Raccoons. Teams stay the same all match.
  3. Map vote. Rounds come in pairs on the same map and layout, so both teams get the same board as thieves and as police. Maps rotate between pairs.
  4. Briefing. A card tells you your role this round and the next.
  5. Preparation (25 seconds). Thieves hide. Police are locked in their staging room and can’t see out.
  6. Play (about 4 minutes). The staging gate opens.
  7. Round result. The winning team scores a point. Then the teams swap roles.
  8. Match result. The first team to the target wins. The host can start a rematch or go back to the lobby.

How a round is won

  • Police win by catching every thief before time runs out.
  • Thieves win if even one of them is still free when time runs out, or if they finish enough tasks to open the exit and every thief who is still free gets out through it.
  • If the last thief is caught on the very last moment, the police win: captures are counted before the clock.

If you are a thief

  • Walk up to a prop and press Q to look exactly like it. R mirrors your disguise to match its neighbours; Z drops it.
  • You can move while disguised, more slowly. A prop that slides around in front of an officer is a giveaway, so freeze when they look your way.
  • Every 25–35 seconds you make a sound: a whistle, or a sound that matches your disguise. A countdown above your head warns you. Police hear roughly where it came from, so think about moving afterwards.
  • E starts a task: disable a security panel, forge an exhibit label, or unlock the loading bay. Stand still for 3–6 seconds; moving, a stun or a capture cancels it. Tasks leave a clue (an opened panel, a swapped label) and the radio names the part of the building.
  • Finish the required tasks (shown at the bottom left) and the exit opens. Get there to escape.
  • F drops a decoy that makes two fake sounds (twice a round). SPACE throws smoke once a round: police can’t see or shoot through it, and you get a burst of speed.

If you are police

  • You start locked in staging during the preparation phase.
  • You see what your team can see. Everything else is dimmed, and nothing there is shown.
  • Aim with the mouse (or the right thumbstick) and click or SPACE to fire. The dashed line shows where a shot goes; walls and smoke stop it.
  • A shot that hits a thief, disguised or not, catches them. A shot that hits a real prop heats your blaster. Get it too hot and it locks for a few seconds.
  • Q or right-click: a stun pulse (once a round). Thieves nearby freeze, lose their task progress, and a stunned disguise visibly wobbles.
  • Sound arrows at the edge of your screen point toward what you heard. Bigger and brighter means closer. They give a direction, never an exact spot.

Maps

  • Grand Museum After Hours: galleries, a sculpture hall, restoration rooms, a security wing, a moonlit courtyard and a loading bay with the way out.
  • Night Market: lantern-lit stalls, a food court, cold storage, back alleys and a delivery yard gate.
  • Seaside Arcade: neon halls, a prize counter, a café, maintenance corridors, a bumper car rink and a boardwalk out to the pier.

Every layout opens and closes some passages, places its props and picks its task spots from a seed, and is checked to be fully connected before you play it.

Bots, joining late and dropping out

  • With bots on, empty seats are filled up to ten, five against five. Bots are labelled in the roster, follow the same rules and swap roles with their team. Police bots only know what a human officer on their team could see.
  • A friend joining mid-match takes over a bot’s seat: its team, its role and where it is right now.
  • If you drop out, a bot keeps your seat warm. Reopen the game in the same tab to take it back with your team and score.
  • Matches live only in the server’s memory: if the server restarts, the match ends.

Accessibility

  • Every important sound has a visual: arrows for sounds, a countdown for your own whistle, the radio box for events.
  • Sound effects and music have separate volume controls (the ♪ button in a match).
  • If your system asks for reduced motion, flashing, wobbling and typing effects are turned off.
  • Phones play in landscape, with large buttons. Turn your phone sideways if you see the rotate screen.
